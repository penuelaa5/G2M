# Messaging Pillars · Roamly Groups

## 1. From/To market shift

- **From, life before:** From: Planning a group trip means one friend quietly becomes the unpaid travel agent. The planning tools (Maps, Wanderlog, the group chat) help you decide but can’t move money. The money tools (Splitwise, Venmo) split a bill but can’t book anything. The two halves never connect, so one person has to be the glue: researching, forcing the decision, booking across a dozen sites, then chasing everyone to get paid back. Most trips never make it out of the chat.
- **To, life after:** To: Deciding and paying finally happen in the same place. The whole group votes, everyone pays their own share at booking, and the trip is locked before the momentum dies. Nobody has to be the glue, and “we should take a trip” actually becomes a booked trip.

This is true even to someone who’s never seen Roamly, and it uses your Despite honestly: the reason the old world stayed broken is that nothing connected the decision to the money. Per-person payment rails are what made the “To” possible.

## 2. Audiences

- **Number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Why you picked this number:** Roamly is a two-sided consumer product, and the two sides use it for opposite reasons. The organizer wants to put the work and the decisions down. The traveler wants a say and wants to owe nobody. One message can’t carry both without collapsing into the “fast, easy app for everyone” mush your Module 3 slides warned about. They share one core truth, the group books together and the trip actually happens, so I write one pillar per side off that truth.

## 3. Messaging pillars

### Audience 1: the organizer (the friend who always ends up running the trip)
- **Value prop:** Get the trip booked without being the group’s travel agent. You stop doing all the research, stop forcing every decision alone, and stop chasing anyone for money, and the trip still happens.
- **Capabilities:** Group voting on activities, so the decisions are shared, not dumped on you.
One place to book the whole itinerary, instead of a dozen tabs.
Each person pays their own share at booking, so you never front the money.
Expenses track and split themselves, no post-trip spreadsheet.
- **Evidence:** 78% of trips started in Roamly got booked, against 34% for groups on the Splitwise-plus-group-chat stack.
Median time from first idea to booked dropped from 11 days to 3.
Organizers saved about 6 hours of coordination per trip and collapsed 4 apps into one.
Organizer NPS of 72 after a single trip.
“I didn’t have to be the mom of the group for once. I actually enjoyed the trip I planned.” — beta organizer, 4-person Lisbon trip.

### Audience 2: the traveler (the friend coming along)
- **Value prop:** Get a real say in the trip and show up owing nothing. You vote on what you actually do, pay only your share, and never get the “you still owe me” text.
- **Capabilities:** Vote on activities and see the group’s choices in the open.
Pay your own share up front, at booking.
A clear record of what you paid for, no Splitwise math later.
One link to join, decide, and pay, instead of getting added to five apps.
- **Evidence:** 92% of invited travelers cast at least one vote, so decisions were genuinely shared.
88% paid their share at booking with zero reminders. The group-chat baseline took an average of 3 nudges per person.
Post-trip settle-ups fell to 0.2 per trip, against 5-plus on Splitwise.
84% of travelers said they felt they had a real say in the plan.
“Nobody Venmo-chased me for a month after. I paid once and forgot about it.” — beta traveler, 6-person Tulum trip.
