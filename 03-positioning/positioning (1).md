# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** Tech-savvy travelers aged 25-35 who travel with friends and want to explore local experiences far more often — and within that group, the trip planner who ends up running it.
- **Who want / need:** ...who are tired of spending hours on research, getting everyone to agree, making the final purchase, and tracking who owes what, all stitched together across Google Maps, Splitwise, and a dozen booking pages.
- **We offer:** Roamly Groups is one place where the trip planner and the travelers coordinate, vote on plans, book and pay for experiences together, and track expenses without the manual math.
- **Because of (value proposition):** ...so the planner does all of it in one place and stops being the group’s researcher, decision-maker, and bookkeeper. The outcome: far less work for the organizer, and a trip that actually gets booked instead of stalling in the group chat.
- **Despite (barriers):** ...despite real build risk on the money layer, where payments and transactions depend on regulated integrations like Plaid, and a destination-and-host library that still needs curation and depth before it covers every group’s trip.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Roamly is the only one that actually gets the trip booked and paid, with the work and the decisions shared across the group instead of dumped on one person. The free stack — Wanderlog, Splitwise, a group chat — stops at planning and hands the organizer a to-do list, not a booked trip.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups is the single place where a travel group votes on plans, books and pays for experiences together, and splits the costs automatically. That frees the planner from being the group’s researcher, decision-maker, and bookkeeper, so the trip actually gets booked instead of stalling in the group chat.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** C · Stranger clarity
- **What it revealed:** The test revealed that the statement is easily understood but not distinctive: the cold reader grasped the core promise instantly, yet their explanation could describe almost any group-travel app, because the product sentence leans on generic verbs and a crowded “one place” claim. Where it felt too broad was “experiences” (scope unclear, activities only or flights and lodging too), too generic was “automatically” hiding the payment mechanic that is the real differentiator, and unclear was the competitive edge, since the reader couldn’t name a single alternative the product beats.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups turns a group trip from an endless group-chat debate into something that’s actually booked and paid for, with the whole group voting on the activities and each person covering their own share. That frees the friend who always ends up running the trip from being its researcher, decision-maker, and bookkeeper, so the plan gets locked in instead of stalling.
